Climate Change Data Analysis

After taking a semester-long environmental science course, I was inspired to build a project that could make the vast, interconnected science of climate change accessible to a general audience. I learned more from that course than almost any other, and I wanted to apply that knowledge to something someone without a scientific background could meaningfully explore. The result is "The Threshold," an interactive web application that maps 19 scientifically documented climate tipping points, the thresholds beyond which a natural system can shift irreversibly. The app lets users explore each tipping point in depth, tracing the cascade of effects that follows when one system tips, from melting ice sheets raising sea levels to collapsing ocean currents disrupting monsoons that feed hundreds of millions of people. A built-in Domino Simulator lets users adjust real-world conditions like emissions pressure, policy response speed, and the spread of misinformation, then watch which tipping points activate and cascade into each other. There are thousands of human activities that contribute to and interact with climate change, but most people are never shown how these forces connect. This project is an attempt to show how climate change actually cascades, why it accelerates, and what makes it so difficult to stop once it begins.
